# qphys — quantum formalism as a modeling language

Two senses of "we do not know what happens next", built as two sub-projects:

| | `collapse` (Project 1) | `lawlearn` (Project 2) |
|---|---|---|
| Unknown | the **outcome** | the **law** |
| Known | the dynamical map | some outcomes (data) |
| Formal object | density matrix + Kraus operators | Lagrangian / metric / potential |
| Success metric | out-of-sample NLL at matched capacity | extrapolation outside the training domain |
| Real-data anchor | Seattle daily precipitation | Mercury's orbit |

---

## The claims table

This is the first thing in this README because of honesty rule 2: **the null
result is a deliverable**. If this table ever stops containing a prominent
statement of what was *not* shown, the project has failed that rule whatever
else it achieved.

| # | Claim | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A quantum-formalism model **predicts** a real series better than the best classical model at matched capacity | **NOT SHOWN — it loses** | `reports/seattle_table.csv`; see below |
| 2 | Quantum causal states **represent** a process with less memory than classical ones | **Holds**, and it is a theorem, not a fit | `C_q <= C_mu` asserted over random chains, `tests/test_acceptance_collapse.py` |
| 3 | A Leggett–Garg violation in a recorded series would be evidence of anything | **FALSE, provably** | `K3 <= 1` is an algebraic identity for an aligned estimator; a classical Gaussian oscillator saturates it exactly at every lag |
| 4 | The speed limit is recoverable from slow motion alone, via the quartic term of `p(v)` | **Holds**, at every speed tested from 0.2c to 0.7c, with a ~40% failure rate | §10 sweep below, 5 seeds per row |
| 5 | That route works on real orbital data | **FALSE** | Mercury's perihelion speed is `1.97e-4 c`, three orders below the identifiability floor |

### Claim 1 in full, because it is the headline

On Seattle daily wet/dry (1461 days, temporal 70/30 split fixed before the
first fit), **the HQMM loses**. On this dataset, at this sample size, the
quantum model does not earn its complexity.

Every stochastic row is a mean over 5 seeds with its standard deviation, and
the numbers live in `reports/seattle_table.csv`, regenerated by the
experiment rather than typed here.

Two things had to be fixed before that sentence was safe to write, and both
moved the answer:

**The classical side was under-tuned.** Without random restarts the HMM at
k = 2 and k = 3 converges to a degenerate single-emission solution and
returns exactly the i.i.d. NLL of 0.9841. With 8 restarts they reach 0.8551
and 0.8620. That makes the classical baseline stronger, which is the
direction that matters -- an easy baseline flatters the quantum model.

**A single fit nearly produced a false reversal.** One hqmm-d3 fit reached
0.8464 bits. At 17 free parameters that beats every classical model costing
17 or fewer (best: hmm-2 at 0.8551), and being beaten only by a *19*-parameter
HMM is not a matched-capacity comparison -- honesty rule 3 is explicit about
that. It looked like the headline was wrong.

Over five seeds the same configuration averages **0.8785 ± 0.0267**, while
hmm-2 sits at **0.8551 ± 0.000009**. The 0.8464 was the favourable tail of a
wide distribution, not a result. The HQMM's test NLL depends strongly on the
restart draw; the small HMM is essentially deterministic. Honesty rule 6 is
what caught this, and it is the clearest illustration in this repo of why
single-seed results are not results.

### The winner is smaller than anything that was being argued about

Seed-averaged, the best model on this data is a **five-parameter HMM** at
0.8551 bits. Every more expensive model is worse:

| model | free params | test NLL | std over 5 seeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hmm-2** | **5** | **0.8551** | 0.000009 |
| hmm-4 | 19 | 0.8563 | 0.0121 |
| markov-3 | 8 | 0.8569 | — |
| hqmm-d2 | 7 | 0.8630 | 0.0000002 |
| hqmm-d3 | 17 | 0.8785 | 0.0267 |

`hmm-4` has nearly four times the capacity of `hmm-2` and is *worse* on
average; its extra parameters buy variance (std 0.0121) rather than fit. The
single-fit table that started this had `hmm-4` as the winner at 0.8459, which
was the same kind of favourable draw as the HQMM's 0.8464.

So the honest summary of this dataset is not "quantum loses to a big
classical model". It is that **1461 days of wet/dry carries about 0.855 bits
per symbol of predictable structure, and five parameters is enough to
capture it** -- after which extra capacity, of either kind, is noise.

### Claims 1 and 2 must never be conflated

| Claim | What it is about | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Representation (2) | an idealised construction from a *known* transition matrix | holds |
| Prediction (1) | *estimation* from finite data | fails |

Keeping these apart is the single most important piece of intellectual
hygiene in this project. A 72% memory saving on the estimated chain and a
worse test NLL are both true at once, and they say different things.

### What is never claimed

**No time series here "is quantum."** The only claim under test is whether a
model built from the quantum formalism is more efficient or more accurate
than the best classical model at matched capacity. That is a modeling claim,
not a physics claim (honesty rule 1).

### Claim 4: what limits `c_hat` is the readout, not the fit

Five seeds per row, converged runs only, with the failure rate beside the
mean because a third of these fits do not converge:

| v_max | noise | converged | c_hat | sd | readout floor | gap to floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 0 | 3/5 | 0.9792 | 0.0092 | 0.9831 | 0.4% |
| 0.3 | 0 | 3/5 | 0.9485 | 0.0024 | 0.9615 | 1.4% |
| 0.5 | 0 | 3/5 | 0.8813 | 0.0022 | 0.8874 | 0.7% |
| 0.5 | 2% | 3/5 | 0.8802 | 0.0030 | 0.8874 | 0.8% |
| 0.7 | 0 | 3/5 | 0.7526 | 0.0007 | 0.7566 | 0.5% |

The **readout floor** is `c_hat` recovered from the EXACT `gamma v` by the
same cubic fit -- the error a perfect momentum function would still incur.
The recovered value tracks it to within 1.4% everywhere, so **effectively all
of the deviation from `c = 1` belongs to the readout, not to the network**.
The network learns `p(v)` well at every speed tested.

Three consequences, each of which contradicts a plausible reading of the raw
`c_hat` column:

- **The quartic IS identifiable below 0.4c.** At 0.2c the converged seeds give
  0.979, within 0.4% of the best that readout can do.
- **0.5c is not a sweet spot.** Its cubic bias (-11%) is worse than 0.3c's
  (-4%). A sweep that reported ~1.00 there was averaging scatter across the
  true value, not measuring it.
- **0.7c is worse than 0.5c for an algebraic reason**, not only a
  conditioning one: the truncation bias grows monotonically with the fitted
  range, from -2% at 0.2c to -24% at 0.7c.

Keeping the `v^5` term trades that bias for variance, and the trade is only
worth it where there is bias to remove:

| v_max | cubic | quintic | better |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | 0.9485 ± 0.0024 (5.2% off) | 0.9407 ± 0.0065 (5.9% off) | cubic |
| 0.5 | 0.8813 ± 0.0022 (11.9% off) | **0.9870 ± 0.0184 (1.3% off)** | quintic |

So the best configuration measured here is **`v_max = 0.5` read with a
quintic**, at `c_hat = 0.9870 ± 0.0184`. The cubic readout is the default
because it is lower variance and it is the method specified; the quintic is
one keyword away.

### The convergence gate had to be fixed before the noisy rows meant anything

The first sweep reported a **100% failure rate** at 5% and 10% noise. That was
the gate, not the fits: injected noise puts a floor under the achievable
training loss -- 2.9e-04, 1.8e-03 and 7.3e-03 at 2%, 5% and 10% -- so a fixed
1e-03 threshold is reachable at 2% and *arithmetically impossible* above it.
The gate now measures the excess over that floor, which keeps honesty rule 7
intact because the floor is fixed by the injection's design, not read off the
answers.

## Honesty rules

Binding, not style notes. This topic attracts nonsense.

1. Never claim a series is quantum — see above.
2. The null result is a deliverable. Falsification machinery is built before models.
3. Match **capacity**, not state count. `common/metrics.free_params` counts it, with the derivation in its docstring.
4. Temporal split, declared before the first fit.
5. Verify, do not assert. Every reported number comes from a cell that ran.
6. ≥5 seeds, with the failure rate reported. `common/seeding.summarise` counts NaNs as failures rather than dropping them.
7. Convergence criteria pre-declared: `seeding.CONVERGENCE_REL_LOSS = 1e-3`, on training loss, never on the answer.

## Layout

```text
src/qphys/
  common/      seeding (rules 6, 7) · units (natural units) · metrics · plotting
  collapse/    kraus · causal_states · leggett_garg · processes · baselines · data
  lawlearn/    relativistic (symbolic) · orbit (acceptance targets) · lawnet
tests/         every VERIFIED number, re-measured on this machine
reports/       generated tables
data/raw/      downloaded once, read-only, checksummed
```

**Natural units everywhere**: `c = m = hbar = 1` internally, converted only at
the reporting boundary. In SI, `c^2 ~ 9e16` and the loss surface is unusable.
Orbits use AU and years for the same reason (`GM_sun ~ 39.48`, not `1.3e20`).

Logic lives in `src/`. Notebooks import it; a notebook is never the source of
truth.
